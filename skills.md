## Academic
Broad theoretical knowledge across a wide range of disciplines - biology, history, law, psychology, military tactics, engineering principles, medicine, chemistry, and more. Academic characters are not necessarily specialists; they are people who have read, studied, and retained enough to form useful hypotheses and recognise patterns across fields. Knowledge doesn't guarantee practical execution, but it informs and guides it. 

For applied, hands-on practical knowledge, see [[Mechanics]].

**Overcome:** Use Academic to recall relevant information, identify a substance, organism, or process, understand a document or technical text, make an educated inference about an unfamiliar situation, or work out what something is and how it probably behaves.

**Create an Advantage:** Use Academic to establish a useful fact as a scene aspect - _Knows the Building's Original Purpose_, _Recognised the Symptoms_, _Behavioural Science 101_, _Hazardous Material Storage_.

**Attack**: An academic understanding of human physiology might conceivably be useful, but only in extremely specific circumstances, or a specific stunt.

**Defend**: Only to maintain a detached, reasonable perspective on human folly as the blows fall.

## Analyse
Methodical, sustained observation and the careful drawing of conclusions. Where [[Instinct]] is quick and intuitive, Analyse is slow and deliberate - spending time with a situation, a space, or a person and building a reliable picture of what is actually happening and why. It takes longer. Its results are more dependable and often deeper. In the context of the ongoing crisis, Analyse is the skill that turns confusing symptoms into actionable understanding.

**Overcome:** Use Analyse to conduct a thorough investigation of a location or situation, work out the underlying cause of a problem, build a profile of a person or group from observed evidence, or establish a reliable conclusion from incomplete information over time.

**Create an Advantage:** Use Analyse to establish a deep, evidenced aspect if you have sufficient time to study the subject - _I Know the Guard Patrol Schedule_, _Favours Their Right Leg_, _Consistent Behavioural Patterns_ - that reflects sustained, systematic attention rather than a first impression.

**Attack**: Due to the time consuming nature of this skill you can't use it to Attack.

**Defend**: Likewise for Defending.

## Craft
The deliberate, skilled making of things from raw materials. Craft takes time and produces reliable results - a functioning bow, a meal for twenty survivors, a set of armour that will actually stop something, a rabbit snare. Unlike [[Improvise]], which is fast and temporary, Craft is the skill of making things that last, function correctly, and can be trusted when the situation demands. While [[Mechanics]] covers repair and operation, Craft is about building from scratch.

**Overcome:** Use Craft to build something from raw components over time - weapons, armour, shelter elements, tools, agricultural equipment, or medical devices - to a quality that will hold up under real use.

**Create an Advantage:** Use Craft to establish a durable aspect on a location or object - _Properly Reinforced Door_, _Weapons Cache, Hidden and Accessible_, _Functional Distillation Rig_ - that represents lasting, quality work rather than a quick fix.

**Attack**: Craft is a long and involved process - longer than a combat round at any rate so can't be used for an Attack.

**Defend**: As above.
### Example Stunts

- **The Right Tool.** When you have time to prepare and access to even minimal raw materials, you may always produce the specific tool, weapon, or item most needed for the next scene, without rolling. It exists because you made it. What it costs in time and materials is for the GM to determine narratively.
- **Built to Last.** Any advantage you create with Craft has its difficulty to remove increased by 1. Your work holds.
- **Structural Eye.** You may use Craft instead of Analyse to assess the defensive qualities of a location - what would hold, what wouldn't, where an individual enemy or hostile group would most likely breach, and what an hour's work could change about that picture.


## Deceive
Fast talk, lying, bluffing, and misdirection under pressure. Deceive is the skill of making someone believe something false in the short term - getting through the next few minutes, past the checkpoint, or out of the immediate confrontation. Deceptions built on Deceive will not withstand close scrutiny or the passage of time, and when they unravel, they unravel badly. For longer-lasting persuasion, see [[Manipulate]].

**Overcome:** Use Deceive to talk your way past immediate suspicion, fabricate a plausible explanation on the spot, impersonate someone convincingly in a brief encounter, or construct a false impression that satisfies someone just long enough.

**Create an Advantage:** Use Deceive to plant a false belief as a scene aspect -  _But You're Meant to be Here_, _Distracted by the Story You Told_, or _Promise of a Good Time_ - that you or allies can invoke until the deception is discovered.

**Attack:** Deceive can be used to make a social attack when a lie is designed to cause immediate distress or undermine someone's confidence in what they know - the verbal equivalent of pulling the rug out. Damages Stress

**Defend**: Deceive isn't good for physically defending yourself.

### Example Stunts

- **Poker Face.** Gain +2 to Deceive when maintaining a false identity or position under direct, pointed questioning. You give nothing away under pressure.
- **Plausible Deniability.** Once per scene, when a deception you have created is challenged or investigated, you may spend a Fate Point to add a piece of supporting detail that makes it more credible, forcing an additional roll to see through it rather than automatically exposing it.
- **The Simplest Story.** You may use Deceive instead of Manipulate when the person you're attempting to convince is already frightened or off-balance. Fear makes people want simple explanations. You have one ready.

## Dexterity
Manual dexterity, fine-motor skills, and physical coordination under pressure. Dexterity covers the use of firearms and ranged weapons, as well as climbing, crawling through tight spaces, picking locks, slipping restraints, and any situation where precise, controlled physical movement is required. It is the skill of doing difficult things with your hands and body without the brute application of force. 

Its contrasting skill is [[Physique]] which relates more to brute force.

**Overcome:** Use Dexterity to scale a fence, crawl through a collapsed tunnel, pick a lock, slip free from restraints, thread through debris-strewn ground without making noise, or make a technically demanding ranged shot. If the obstacle requires nimbleness or precision rather than strength, Dexterity is the appropriate skill.

**Create an Advantage:** Use Dexterity to get into a superior position before others know you're there, or to create an obstacle during a chase. Establish aspects such as _Better Vantage Point_, _Already Through the Window_, or _Trip hazard_ on yourself or a scene.

**Attack:** Dexterity is used to make ranged attacks with firearms and thrown weapons. The precision and coordination of the shot, not the weapon's raw power, is what this skill reflects.

**Defend:** Dexterity is used to dodge physical attacks - stepping aside from a thrown object, ducking a swing, or breaking clear of a grab before it becomes a grapple.

### Example Stunts

- **Muscle Memory.** When you succeed with style on a Dexterity attack with a firearm, you may reduce the target's next defence roll by 1 in addition to any other effect, as your follow-up shot is already mid-arc. The target is _Off-Balance_, not just hit.
- **Dead Bolt.** You've picked enough locks under pressure that the mechanism barely slows you down. Gain +2 to Dexterity when overcoming a physical lock, hatch, or restraint in a situation where time or stress is a factor.
- **Squeeze Through.** Once per scene, when navigating a physical environment - collapsing structure, debris field, tight crawlspace - you may declare you are through or past it with no roll, provided you had any plausible chance of making it. You simply do.


## Dominate
The capacity to fill a space - to command attention, assert presence, and make yourself impossible to ignore. Dominate is the opposite of [[Stealth]]: it is the skill of being the loudest, most visible, most compelling thing in the room, whether through force of personality, volume, physical authority, or sheer refusal to be overlooked. In a crisis, Dominate is what holds a crowd together, breaks a standoff, or creates the distraction that everyone else needs.

**Overcome:** Use Dominate to take command of a chaotic group, be heard and heeded above competing noise, stare down an immediate physical threat, command an animal, or project enough authority that someone backs down.

**Create an Advantage:** Use Dominate to establish aspects on a scene that reflect your presence having changed the social or physical dynamics - _Nobody Is Moving_, _All Eyes On You_, _He's Not Sure Enough of Himself to Push It_.

**Attack:** Dominate can be used to make a social or psychological attack - an act of pure, directed intimidation that creates mental stress.

**Defend**: By default, you can't defend with Dominate, except via an appropriate stunt.
### Example Stunts

- **Rally Point.** Once per scene, when a group of people - PC or NPC - is about to break under pressure, you may spend a Fate Point and use Dominate to stop it. Everyone present treats their next action as if they have one additional free invoke. You have made them feel, for a moment, that someone knows what they're doing.
- **Standing Order.** When you successfully use Dominate to give an instruction to a group of NPCs, that instruction holds for the rest of the scene without needing reinforcement. You do not need to keep watching them.
- **Enough.** You may use Dominate instead of Resolve to defend against attempts to intimidate or demoralise you. The attack simply finds nothing to grip. You are the bigger presence in the room and both of you know it.


## Endure
Physical resilience - the body's capacity to weather punishment, deprivation, disease, and exhaustion. Endure is not about avoiding damage but absorbing it: pushing through pain, continuing to function on no sleep, resisting the early stages of infection, pressing on after an injury that would stop most people. In a world where access to medical care is collapsing, Endure becomes one of the most important survival skills a character can have. It is the counterpart to [[Resolve]], which covers the mind.

**Overcome:** Use Endure to push through physical pain or injury; to resist the effects of hunger, thirst, or exposure over time; to shake off the onset of illness; to continue functioning under conditions that are physiologically hostile.

**Defend:** Endure may be used to defend against attacks that operate through physical attrition rather than direct force - prolonged exposure, toxins, or conditions that wear the body down.

**Attack**: It would have be a very extreme situation to use Endure for an Attack, but it would be memorable.

**Defend**: Assuming that the attack is one that is mostly to inflict pain (bar fight) rather than lasting, inhibiting damage (gunshot), then Endure could be used to Defend.
### Example Stunts

- **Tough as Nails**. Once per session, at the cost of a fate point, you can reduce the severity of a moderate consequence that’s physical in nature to a mild consequence (if your mild consequence slot is free), or erase a mild consequence altogether.
- **Running on Empty.** Once per session, you may clear one physical stress box without any treatment, narrating it as a willed physical response - forcing function from a body that has decided it has had enough. This does not remove consequences.
- **Iron Stomach.** You do not make Endure rolls to handle food and water of questionable quality or safety, provided it is not acutely toxic. You have eaten worse. The things that sicken others do not slow you down.


## Improvise
The ability to jury-rig, bodge, and make do. Improvise covers rapid, temporary solutions constructed from whatever is available - hotwiring a vehicle, fashioning a crude weapon, rigging a door shut, building a basic trap, or fashioning a disguise from bin bags and gaffer tape. The results work, for now, under these conditions. They are not elegant and they will not last. For considered, reliable construction, see [[Craft]].

**Overcome:** Use Improvise to build a functional temporary solution to an immediate problem using available materials, carry out a rapid repair that will hold under pressure for a limited time, or turn an unpromising object into something useful right now.

**Create an Advantage:** Use Improvise to establish a situational aspect - _Crude Barricade_, _Makeshift Firestarter_, _Trip-Wire Rigged Across the Doorway_ - that provides a meaningful tactical or practical advantage in the current scene.

**Attack:** An improvised weapon - a broken chair leg, a length of pipe, a sharpened stake - can be used to attack. When using Improvise to attack with a weapon you have just constructed, the attack uses Improvise rather than Physique.

**Defend**: Providing the scene supports the intention, you can Defend yourself by using a human shield or pulling a makeshift shield in front of you, or the classic cutting of the right rope to be whisked away to safety by a convenient pulley system. You get this just once.
### Example Stunts

- **Found Art.** When creating an improvised weapon or tool, you may spend a Fate Point to have it function as well as a properly manufactured equivalent for the duration of the current scene, with no degradation penalty.
- **Rapid Fix.** Gain +2 to Improvise when carrying out a repair or bodge under time pressure - a countdown, pursuit, or immediate threat. Urgency sharpens your focus rather than scattering it.
- **Controlled Demolition.** When you have at least a few minutes and access to flammable or otherwise unstable materials, you may use Improvise to create a _Rigged to Blow_ aspect on any object, vehicle, or structural element in the scene. When that aspect is invoked to trigger the effect, it counts as a Fair (+2) attack against everything in the zone. You set it off, or someone else does, or it goes off at the wrong moment - but it goes off.


## Instinct

The gut feeling that something is wrong. Instinct combines conscious and subconscious perception - the hairs on your neck, the shadow that moved slightly wrong, the hesitation in someone's voice that doesn't fit what they're saying. Instinct is fast, dirty, and not always right, but it is often the difference between being ready and being caught. It is also the primary defence against attempts to [[Deceive]] you.

**Overcome:** Use Instinct to notice something hidden or concealed, pick up on an immediate environmental threat, clock that someone is being deceptive or withholding information, or react to an ambush before it fully lands.

**Create an Advantage:** Use Instinct to read a situation or a person quickly - _Something Is Wrong Here_, _She's Holding Something Back_, _He Knows We're Coming_ - establishing aspects that reflect immediate perceptual judgment.

**Attack**: Your Instinct has already triggered by the time you Attack.

**Defend:** Instinct is the primary defence against Deceive. You sense the lie before you can articulate why. It may also be used in the case of an ambush.
### Example Stunts

- **Sixth Sense**. You have an almost preternatural capacity for detecting danger. Your Instinct skill works unimpeded by conditions like total concealment, darkness, or other sensory impairments in situations where someone or something intends to harm you.
- **Never Quite Relaxed.** You are never considered _unaware_ for the purposes of an ambush unless you are physically incapacitated or asleep. You may always make an Instinct roll to notice something is wrong before it lands, even in apparently safe circumstances.
- **Trust the Feeling.** Once per scene, you may act on an Instinct-based hunch - moving away from a position, refusing to open a door, insisting on a different route - without explanation. If this turns out to be correct, you gain a free invoke on a relevant aspect. If it turns out to be wrong, you generate a boost for the GM.


## Manipulate
Persuasion, seduction, and sustained influence. Manipulate is the skill of bringing another person around to your way of thinking through deliberate, targeted engagement - not trickery, but pressure applied at exactly the right point. Its effects last. When someone acts against their better judgement because you convinced them to, that decision tends to stay made. When they discover they've been played, the damage is lasting - or they may double down.

This is the longer-lasting alternative to [[Deceive]], but demands a little more time and commitment.

**Overcome:** Use Manipulate to convince someone to help you, give you access, or act against mild self-interest; to negotiate, bargain, or establish trust over the course of a scene; to talk someone down from an extreme emotional state.

**Create an Advantage:** Use Manipulate to build a scene aspect representing established trust, obligation, or emotional leverage - _Feels Like She Owes You_, *Agitated Crowd*, _Already Agreed in Principle_.

**Attack**: You can't manipulate a person into suffering harm, or at least not in the moment.

**Defend**: Likewise, your Machiavellian schemes are not going to block that punch- ouch!

### Example Stunts

- **You Remind Me of Someone.** Once per scene, you may learn one of a target NPC's aspects - something they care about, fear, or want - by engaging them in brief, apparently casual conversation. No roll required; this is purely observational. The GM provides an aspect, which you can invoke normally.
- **Appeal to What Matters.** Gain +2 to Manipulate when you know at least one of the target's aspects and frame your appeal explicitly around it. Knowing what people care about makes you dangerous.
- **The Long Game.** When you successfully create a lasting Manipulate-based advantage on a character, that aspect may be invoked once for free by any member of your group, not just you. You have turned someone into a shared resource.


## Mechanics
A practical, intuitive understanding of how things work - mechanical systems, tools, vehicles, common electronics, and the physical infrastructure of daily life. Mechanics covers hotwiring a van, rigging a generator, repairing a water pump, bypassing an electronic lock with the right tools, and diagnosing why the heating system has stopped working. It is hands-on applied knowledge. For theoretical or highly specialist understanding, see [[Academic]].

**Overcome:** Use Mechanics to repair or operate a mechanical or electrical system, diagnose a fault and work out a fix, drive or operate a vehicle in normal conditions, or bypass a mechanical or low-level electronic security measure.

**Create an Advantage:** Use Mechanics to identify a fault that can be exploited - an engine that could be quietly sabotaged, a lock that's already compromised, a building whose systems could be rigged in your favour. Create aspects like _Fuel Line Disconnected_, _Emergency Exit Unlocked_, or _Generator on a Timer_.

**Attack**: Sabotage or activate a conveniently placed piece of machinery. Likely single-use.

**Defend**: Similar to Attack, but a little harder to justify. However if it fits the fiction, then it's up to the GM.

### Example Stunts

- **MacGyver Protocol.** When using Mechanics to repair or rig something with inadequate tools or materials, you do not suffer the usual penalty for improvising under constraint, provided you have at least something to work with. You've learned to treat those limitations as design specifications.
- **Knows What It's Doing.** Gain +2 to Mechanics when diagnosing the fault or failure mode of a mechanical or electrical system, as distinct from fixing it. Understanding the problem is a distinct and valuable skill.
- **Hot Start.** You may use Mechanics instead of Dexterity to operate a vehicle under pressure - coaxing a failing engine, controlling a skid, or keeping a damaged vehicle moving. Your technical understanding becomes a physical asset.

## Preparedness
Forethought, readiness, and the habit of anticipating what might be needed before the need arises. Preparedness is the counterpart to [[Scrounge]]: where [[Scrounge]] finds things in the moment, Preparedness reflects having already put them there. It covers pre-planned equipment caches, contingency thinking, established procedures for likely emergencies, and the capacity to produce a solution that you had the foresight to prepare. It is also the skill of planning ahead - routes scouted, exits noted, resources mapped.

**Overcome:** Use Preparedness to produce an item, piece of information, or resource that your character could plausibly have prepared or positioned in advance, without requiring an in-scene search. The roll establishes whether your planning was adequate, not whether the item exists.

**Create an Advantage:** Use Preparedness to establish prior planning as a scene aspect - _Already Scouted the Route_, _Supply Cache at the Rendezvous_, _Backup Key Hidden Before We Left_, _Signal Agreed in Advance_ - that your group can invoke during the current situation.

**Attack**: Preparedness gives the the best resources to fight with, but can't make the Attack itself.

**Defend**: If you're walking into a gun fight and you've prepared by fitting your shirt with an iron plate, then you can have a single Defend action from Preparedness.
### Example Stunts

- **Contingency Plan.** Once per session, when a situation goes wrong in a specific way you could reasonably have anticipated, you may declare that you prepared for exactly this. Produce one relevant aspect or resource without a roll. The GM may ask you to briefly describe your preparation. It must be plausible, not miraculous.
- **Kit Check.** At the start of any scene involving significant risk, you may make a Preparedness roll against Fair (+2) difficulty. On a success, establish one free-invokable aspect representing prior preparation relevant to the scene - something you loaded into the bag, the thing you checked before leaving, the route you already know. This is your standard operating procedure.
- **Check Your Other Pocket.** Gain +2 to Preparedness when testing to see if a companion has a specific piece of useful equipment. It's their item - you just made sure they remembered it.


## Resolve
Mental fortitude. The capacity to keep functioning when the situation is overwhelming - when what you have just seen is incomprehensible, when the task is endless, when hope is genuinely difficult to locate. Resolve is not the absence of fear or grief; it is the ability to continue despite them. In a setting where the scale of loss is total and unrelenting, Resolve is the skill that keeps a character operational. It is the counterpart to [[Endure]], which covers the body; Resolve covers the mind.

**Overcome:** Use Resolve to push through fear, panic, or despair in the moment; to hold together under sustained psychological pressure; to keep functioning in the aftermath of a mental or emotional shock; to resist coercion, interrogation, or the slow erosion of hope.

**Create an Advantage**: You can use Resolve to place aspects on yourself, representing a state of deep concentration or focus.

**Attack**: Endure isn’t really used for attacks.

**Defend:** Resolve defends against mental attacks and social actions designed to intimidate, demoralise, or psychologically destabilise. It is also the primary defence against horror - the moment of confronting something that the human mind is not designed to process.

**Special**: The Resolve skill gives you additional mental [stress boxes or consequence slots](https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/stress-consequences). Average (+1) or Fair (+2) gives you a 3-point stress box. Good (+3) or Great (+4) gives you a 3-point and a 4-point stress box. Superb (+5) and above give you an additional mild consequence slot along with the additional stress boxes. This slot can only be used for mental harm.

### Example Stunts

- **Eyes Open.** You do not take mental stress from the first genuinely horrifying thing you witness in any given scene. You have seen enough by now that the initial shock no longer lands the same way. Subsequent horrors in the same scene affect you normally.
- **Not Today.** Once per session, when you would take a moderate or severe mental consequence, you may downgrade it by one step. You are not unaffected. You are simply not broken yet.
- **Anchor.** You may use Resolve to defend on behalf of another character in the same scene who is facing a mental attack or horror response, provided you are present and engaged. You become the fixed point they hold on to. On a success, you absorb the stress instead.



## Scrounge
The ability to find, acquire, and extract useful things from an environment that most people would dismiss as picked clean. Scrounge is the skill of the survivor who comes back with what was needed when nobody believed anything was left. It covers foraging for food in the countryside, searching a building for overlooked resources, finding a working vehicle in a car park full of dead ones, and identifying the thing that everyone else missed. It pairs well with [[Improvise]] and [[Craft]], which can turn raw materials into something usable.

**Overcome:** Use Scrounge to locate specific resources in an environment - food, medicine, fuel, tools, usable materials - in conditions of genuine scarcity. The difficulty reflects how depleted the environment is and how specific the need.

**Create an Advantage:** Use Scrounge to establish a resource aspect for the group - _Found a Working Generator_, _Three Days of Tinned Food, Overlooked_, _Fuel in the Back of the Depot Van_ - that becomes available for the current or subsequent scene.

**Attack**: Scrounge after the Attack, not before it.

**Defend**: Not normally used to Defend.
### Example Stunts

- **Second Pass.** Once per location, after another character has already Scrounged unsuccessfully, you may try again without penalty. You look in different places, using different assumptions. The well is not always as dry as it looks.
- **Forager's Sense.** Gain +2 to Scrounge when looking for food, water, or biological materials in outdoor or semi-outdoor environments. You know what's edible, where water collects, and which plants are useful.
- **I'll Take That.** Once per encounter, you may use Scrounge to defend against a melee attack. If you succeed, the weapon is now yours.


## Stealth
Moving without being seen or heard, and concealing objects and information in ways that resist discovery. Stealth is the art of not being there, or of being there in such a way that nobody notices. The skill of being quiet and small and unremarkable, when that is the only viable option.

**Overcome:** Use Stealth to move through a space undetected, conceal an object on your person or in a location, pass a guard or threat without engaging it, or hide effectively under pursuit.

**Create an Advantage:** Use Stealth to establish positional aspects before a conflict begins - _Unseen Behind the Counter_, _Already In the Room_, _Nobody Knows I'm Here_ - that let you set thlaMe terms of an encounter rather than react to them.

**Attack**: You can only Attack _from_ Stealth, not _with_ it.

**Defend**: Not normally used to Defend, except via a stunt
### Example Stunts

- **Slippery Target**. Provided you’re in darkness, shadow or otherwise obscured conditions, you can use Stealth to defend against Shoot attacks from enemies that are at least one zone away.
- **Ghost Route.** Once per scene, you may move from one zone to any non-adjacent zone without being observed, provided there is any plausible path connecting them. You do not have to specify how. You are simply there.
- **Hidden in Plain Sight.** You may use Stealth to conceal an object in a location rather than on your person. When you do, establish a difficulty for its discovery equal to your roll result. Anyone searching for the object must overcome that difficulty, even if they know an object is hidden somewhere in the space.
